Lacunar Stroke & Cerebral Small Vessel Disease (CSVD)

Bedside diagnostic criteria, clinical syndromes, and evidence-based secondary prevention for small penetrating artery strokes.

Vascular Pathologies
  • Arteriolosclerosis (40–150 μm): Media thickening and luminal narrowing driven by chronic HTN and aging.
  • Lipohyalinosis (40–200 μm): Fibrinoid necrosis and disorganized collagen deposition in small penetrating arteries.
  • Branch Atheromatous Disease (200–800 μm): Microatheroma at the ostium or proximal segment of a larger branch.
Genetics & Rare CSVD Etiologies
  • CADASIL: AD NOTCH3 mutation. Subcortical white matter hyperintensities (temporal pole hallmark), migraines, dementia.
  • CARASIL: AR HTRA1 mutation. Early alopecia, spondylosis, and cognitive decline.
  • RVCL-S: AD TREX1 mutation. Retinopathy, progressive leukodystrophy, and systemic manifestations (Raynaud's, cirrhosis).

Classic Lacunar Clinical Syndromes

Syndrome Clinical Presentation Neuroanatomical Localization Est. PPV
Pure Motor Hemiparesis Contralateral hemiparesis affecting Face, Arm, and Leg equally. *No cortical signs* (no aphasia, neglect, hemianopia). Internal Capsule (posterior limb), Pons (basis pontis), or Corona Radiata. 52% – 85%
Pure Sensory Syndrome Contralateral numbness/paresthesia affecting Face, Arm, and Leg. Midline split extension. Cheiro-oral-pedal variants. Thalamus (VPL/VPM nuclei), pons, or corona radiata. 95% – 100%
Sensorimotor Syndrome Contralateral motor weakness AND sensory deficit in Face, Arm, and Leg. Thalamocapsular junction or pons. 51% – 87%
Dysarthria-Clumsy Hand Severe dysarthria and dysphagia, with contralateral hand clumsiness and mild facial weakness. Internal Capsule (anterior limb or genu) or Basis Pontis. 96%
Ataxic Hemiparesis Ipsilateral mild hemiparesis with disproportionate cerebellar ataxia in the same limbs. Ataxia exceeds paresis. Pons (basis pontis), Internal Capsule (posterior limb), or corona radiata. 59% – 95%

Secondary Prevention & SPS3 Trial Evidence

SPS3: Antiplatelet Fact Sheet

• **Design**: 3,020 patients with MRI-confirmed lacunar stroke. DAPT (ASA + Clopidogrel) vs. ASA alone.
• **Efficacy**: Recurrent stroke was **not** significantly reduced (HR 0.92, 95% CI 0.72–1.16).
• **Harm**: DAPT significantly increased **major hemorrhage** and **mortality** (HR 1.52, p<0.001).
• **Takeaway**: Routine long-term DAPT is contraindicated for lacunes. Use single antiplatelet long-term.

SPS3: Blood Pressure Fact Sheet

• **Design**: SBP Target <130 mmHg vs. 130–149 mmHg.
• **Recurrent Stroke**: HR 0.81 (95% CI 0.64–1.03), trending toward benefit (19% reduction).
• **ICH Rate**: **Highly significantly reduced** (HR 0.37, 95% CI 0.15–0.95), showing massive protection against hemorrhagic stroke.
• **Takeaway**: Target SBP <130 mmHg long-term for small vessel disease prevention (Class I, LOE B-R).

Bedside Care Targets for Small Vessel Disease: 1. **Blood Pressure**: Long-term target SBP <130 mmHg (SPS3). Gradual reduction in acute phase if needed.
2. **Statin Therapy**: High-intensity statin targeting LDL-C <70 mg/dL (SPARCL).
3. **Diet**: Mediterranean diet with strict Sodium restriction (HTN control).
4. **Capsular Warning Syndrome**: Recurrent brief TIAs with motor symptoms. Extension of microthrombosis. Consider brief hemodynamic support, but avoid long-term DAPT unless symptomatic intracranial stenosis is confirmed.
For educational & clinical decision support. Verify local institutional protocols prior to application.
