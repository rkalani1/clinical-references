Critical Appraisal & Trial Interpretation

Step 1

Baseline Prognostic Balance

Ensure patient cohorts start with the same prognosis. Look at Table 1 for known confounders and verify enroller allocation concealment to prevent investigator selection bias.

Key Questions:
  • Was randomization truly computer-generated?
  • Were recruiters blinded to the next allocation?
  • Were baseline characteristics statistically comparable?
Step 2

Maintained Balance & Blinding

Ensure prognostic balance is maintained during the study. Double-blinding of patients, caregivers, and outcome adjudicators is essential to prevent performance and detection biases.

Key Dangers:
  • Co-interventions (e.g. ICU steroids) unequal between groups.
  • Surgical/device trials where sham control is lacking.
  • Unblinding due to distinct side effects (e.g. CGRP mAbs).
Step 3

Intention-to-Treat (ITT)

Analyze patients in the groups to which they were randomized, regardless of nonadherence, protocol deviations, or crossovers. This preserves baseline prognostic balance.

ITT vs. Per-Protocol:
  • ITT: Minimizes bias; represents real-world efficacy.
  • Per-Protocol: Overestimates effect; prone to attrition bias.
  • Missing data >20% severely compromises trial validity.
Step 4

Efficacy & NNT Calculator

Determine the magnitude (Absolute Risk Reduction) and precision (Confidence Intervals) of the treatment effect. Calculate the Number Needed to Treat (NNT).

Bedside Precision Helper:
ARR: 5.0% NNT: 20
Step 5

Clinical Generalizability

Confirm the study population matches your bedside patient. Check inclusion/exclusion parameters, and confirm that surrogate/intermediate outcomes translate to patient-centered clinical endpoints.

surrogate vs. Hard Endpoints:
  • Surrogate: MRI lesion burden, angiographic recanalization.
  • Hard Endpoint: 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0-2 shift.
  • Trial populations are selected to maximize benefit & minimize harm.
