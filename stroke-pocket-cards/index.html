IV Thrombolysis (IVT) Consent Guidelines

Consent Requirements: Neurology obtains consent for thrombolytic therapy (not for thrombectomy - we assist ED in obtaining family contacts).
Do NOT delay lytics. If the patient lacks capacity and no family/surrogate is available, treat immediately as standard of care.

Key Risks, Benefits & Alternatives

Tenecteplase (TNK) dissolves blood clots to restore cerebral perfusion, significantly improving the probability of a good functional recovery when given within 4.5h.

Consent Discussion Scripts

Standard Window: "GIST" Version (0–4.5 Hours)
"Getting this clot buster after a stroke reduces your risk of permanent disability. People who get this have a much better chance of recovering and getting back to normal activities compared to those who do not. All medicines have risk. With this one, there is a 4% risk of serious bleeding in the brain. However, time is critical. The faster we give it, the greater the chance of a good outcome."
Extended Window Script (4.5–24 Hours)
"Tenecteplase, a clot-dissolving medication, is a treatment for your suspected stroke that can be given beyond the usual 4.5-hour window when brain imaging shows there is still tissue that can be saved. In clinical trials, people who received this treatment in the extended time window had about a 9–11% better chance of recovering without disability compared with people who did not receive the treatment. However, all medicines have some risk, and with clot-dissolving drugs, there is a risk of serious bleeding. Bleeding into the brain that leads to new symptoms can occur in up to about 3% of those treated. This use of clot-dissolving medications beyond 4.5 hours from stroke onset is supported by limited evidence from clinical trials and many uncertainties remain. Your alternatives include standard stroke care without thrombolysis. Based on your imaging findings and clinical presentation, we suspect the benefit of treatment outweighs the risk and recommend proceeding with Tenecteplase treatment."
Usage Guide: Read or paraphrase this script to the patient or legally authorized representative when obtaining consent for Tenecteplase administration in the 4.5 to 24-hour window. Document the discussion and consent decision in the EHR note.

Orolingual Angioedema Protocol

Emergency management of orolingual angioedema associated with IV thrombolytic administration for acute ischemic stroke.

COR IIb
LOE C-EO
Maintain airway
Endotracheal intubation may not be necessary if edema is limited to anterior tongue and lips.
Edema involving larynx, palate, floor of mouth, or oropharynx with rapid progression (within 30 min) poses higher risk of requiring intubation.
Awake fiberoptic intubation is optimal. Nasal-tracheal intubation may be required but poses risk of epistaxis after IV alteplase. Cricothyroidotomy is rarely needed and also problematic after IV alteplase.
Discontinue IV thrombolytic and hold ACE inhibitors
Administer IV methylprednisolone 125 mg
Administer IV diphenhydramine 50 mg
Administer ranitidine 50 mg IV or famotidine 20 mg IV
If there is further increase in angioedema, administer epinephrine (0.1%) 0.3 mL subcutaneously or by nebulizer 0.5 mL
Plasma-derived C1 esterase inhibitor (Berinert) (20IU/kg)
Refer to Epic Order set and choose IV Thrombolytic option:
Life-Threatening Angioedema Treatment (Single Response)
Supportive care
ACE indicates angiotensin-converting enzyme; AIS, acute ischemic stroke; COR, class of recommendation; IV, intravenous; and LOE, Level of Evidence.

Post-Lytic Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH) Protocol

Suspicion of Intracranial Hemorrhage
Prompted by sudden neurological deterioration, new headache, acute hypertension, nausea, or vomiting
Stop thrombolytic if still running
  1. Arrange STAT non-contrast CT Head
  2. STAT blood Draw (while arranging CT Head)
    • Emergency Hemorrhage Panel (PT/INR, platelet count, fibrinogen)
    • Complete coagulation screen (PTT, TT, D-dimers)
    • CBC, type and cross
  3. Call Transfusion Support Services (4-3088 / 8-8700)
    • Order 2 pools Cryoprecipitate immediately (takes ~15-20min to obtain)
  4. Notify patient's family of change in status
CT Scan Delayed?
(i.e. if you expect results in > 30min)
Yes
Empirically administer 2 pools Cryoprecipitate over 10-30min if fibrinogen level < 200
Intracranial blood on CT Head?
Give 2 pools Cryoprecipitate infused over 10-30 min
  1. Order Tranexamic acid STAT and administer 1g IV infused over 10 min
  2. Repeat Emergency Hemorrhage Panel
  3. Call Stroke/Neurology Attending
  4. Consult Neurosurgery if needed
Update family again
  1. If labs remain abnormal on repeat testing or bleeding remains uncontrolled, consult with Hematology Attending
  2. Repeat Emergency Hemorrhagic Panel q4h until normal
Do NOT restart lytic.
End Algorithm
Consider other causes (extending infarct, seizure, hypoxia, hypercarbia, hypoglycemia, electrolytes)
*If Cryo started + infusing and CTH shows NO ICH, STOP infusion

Spontaneous Intraparenchymal Hemorrhage

Reversal Guide for Warfarin

For guideline updates and info on managing bleeds: Stroke Reference Kit Online
For all patients:
  • 1) Order STAT EMERGENCY STROKE PANEL (includes PT/INR, PTT, HB/HCT, PLT and DOAC screen)
  • 2) Obtain history about use of antithrombotic agents (especially anticoagulants), including date/time of last dose
  • 3) If crash craniotomy is considered, TYPE AND SCREEN - STAT and request 2 units emergency-release RBCs
IF ON WARFARIN
Immediately give vitamin K 10mg IV
INR ≥ 1.6
Low evidence: INR 1.3-1.5, consider on case-by-case basis
PCC (Kcentra)
  • PCC (Kcentra) 2000 units IVPB x1
  • Check PT/INR 30min, then every 6 hrs for 24 hrs after completion of PCC.
  • If INR > 1.5 at 30 min, page Hematology and consider giving another 500 units PCC.
  • If INR > 1.5 at 24 hrs, repeat vitamin K 10mg IV over 30 min.
PCC = Prothrombin Complex Concentrate; preferred agent is Kcentra
Absolute contraindications to PCC = DIC or HIT
Relative contraindications (apply to both PCC and FFP)
  • History of thrombotic or thromboembolic event in past 6 weeks (DVT/PE, ischemic stroke, ACS, acute venous/arterial ischemia, etc.)
  • Known prothrombotic condition (malignancy, hypercoagulable condition, hepatic disease, polytrauma, etc.)
  • Major surgery within 6 weeks
Plasma (FFP) Pathway (if PCC unavailable)
  1. Immediately place prepare and transfuse orders for 4 units emergency-release plasma, send a STAT type and screen, and request an additional 4 units.
    (Note: ideal plasma dose is 15mL/kg and each unit is 250-300mL, so some patients may need additional doses). Consider furosemide if patient has CHF
  2. Upon completion of infusion, repeat STAT Emergency Stroke Panel.
  3. If INR still > 1.5, give the 4 U FFP/Plasma.
  4. Upon completion, repeat STAT Emergency Stroke Panel.
  5. If INR still > 1.5 consult hematology.
  6. Repeat INR every 6 hrs for 24 hrs.
  7. If INR remains or increases to > 1.5, repeat vitamin K 10mg IV over 30 min at 24 hours and consult hematology.
