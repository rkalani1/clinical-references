Neuroprognostication

AHA/ASA 2022 Guideline: Postpone Early DNR Orders
Postpone new DNR orders until at least the second full day of inpatient stay (Class IIa, LOE C-LD).

Early care limitations are a strong independent predictor of poor outcomes. Early withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment (WOLST) creates a "self-fulfilling prophecy" where predicted mortality is guaranteed by withdrawing care, rather than reflecting true biological prognosis.

ICH Prognostication

  • • ICH Score Limitations: Highly validated for 30-day mortality (see Calculators). However, it overestimates mortality when aggressive care is provided, as it was derived in populations with high early DNR rates.
  • • FUNC Score for Independence: Predicts 90-day functional independence (mRS ≤3). Use FUNC to balance family discussion with positive, objective framing.
  • • Volume Trajectory: Hematoma expansion occurs mostly in the first 3-6 hours. Stable or shrinking volume on repeat CT at 6h carries a significantly better prognosis.
  • • Location & Size: Lobar and small deep (<30 mL) hemorrhages have the highest recovery potential. Infratentorial (brainstem) and large deep (>60 mL) carry the highest risk of severe disability.

Severe AIS Prognostication

  • • Early NIHSS Limitations: Immediate post-reperfusion NIHSS is not predictive of long-term functional recovery. Dramatic delayed neurological improvements (weeks to months) are common.
  • • ASPECTS & Recanalization: Patients with ASPECTS ≥6 who undergo successful endovascular recanalization (TICI 2b/3) achieve functional independence (mRS 0-2) in up to 50% of cases, despite high initial NIHSS.
  • • Malignant MCA Infarction: Decompressive hemicraniectomy within 48h (age <60) has an NNT of 2 for survival and 4 for functional recovery (mRS 0-3) based on pooled DECIMAL/DESTINY/HAMLET trials.
  • • Basilar Artery Occlusion: Endovascular therapy (ATTENTION/BAOCHE trials) significantly improves functional outcomes even in extended windows. Do not assume clinical futility early.
Clinical Framing Rule
"We are early. The brain needs time. Let us provide aggressive medical care for 72 hours, then re-evaluate the trajectory together."
Ensures that families are not forced into making premature life-or-death decisions when cerebral edema is at its peak and the ultimate recovery capacity is still unknown.

Family Communication Guide

Bedside communication frameworks to assist families in navigating high-stakes medical decisions during acute stroke admissions.

1. The "Best / Realistic / Worst" Case Framework

Instead of presenting a single, arbitrary percentage of recovery, describe the spectrum of possible clinical scenarios. This manages expectations while preserving hope.

Best Case Scenario: "With aggressive rehab and time, he may regain the ability to walk with a cane and communicate basic needs, though some arm weakness will remain."
Most Realistic Outcome: "He will likely require assistance with daily tasks (dressing, transfers) and may need long-term feeding tube support due to swallowing deficits."
Worst Case Scenario: "The swelling could continue to worsen, leading to loss of consciousness or death. If he survives, he may remain completely bedbound and dependent."

Key Bedside Communication Pearls

  • Avoid Subjective Labels: Do not use words like "devastating" or "catastrophic." Instead, use clear, functional descriptions: "The stroke has damaged the area of the brain responsible for speech and movement of the right side."
  • Focus on Patient Values: Ask: "What would [Patient Name] consider an acceptable quality of life? If she could sit at this table and tell us, what would she say about this situation?"
  • Define What We WILL Do: Shift the focus from "withdrawing care" to choosing a pathway of comfort. Explain: "We are continuing aggressive medical monitoring, medication adjustments, and therapy, but focusing on comfort rather than invasive surgeries."
  • Structure the Timeline: Schedule formal updates at predetermined intervals (e.g., 24h, 72h) rather than frequent, ad-hoc discussions. This reduces family anxiety and gives the brain time to show its clinical trajectory.
  • Document Structured Summaries: Write an explicit note in the chart: who was present (including phone/video links), specific functional outcomes discussed, family questions, and agreed-upon step-wise goals of care.
