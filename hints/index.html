HINTS Bedside Exam

Acute Vestibular Syndrome (AVS) Criteria
Target Population: Rapid assessment tool for patients presenting with Acute Vestibular Syndrome (AVS):
• Acute, continuous vertigo lasting >24 hours
• Spontaneous or gaze-evoked nystagmus
• Nausea, vomiting, and head-motion intolerance
• Unsteady gait (often unable to stand without support)

The 3-Step HINTS Clinical Protocol

Sensitivity 96.8%, specificity 98.5% for differentiating central (stroke) from peripheral (vestibular neuritis) lesions. More sensitive than MRI DWI in first 24-48 hours.

H — Head Impulse

Patient fixates on your nose. Gently but rapidly rotate the head 10-20° horizontally.

Normal = CENTRAL
Abnormal = PERIPHERAL

• Normal VOR (no catch-up saccade) points to brainstem/cerebellar stroke.
• Abnormal VOR (delayed catch-up saccade) suggests vestibular neuritis.

N — Nystagmus

Observe eyes in primary gaze, then looking left and right. Note nystagmus beating direction.

Dir-Changing = CENTRAL
Unidirectional = PERIPHERAL

• Direction-changing (beats left on left gaze, right on right gaze) or vertical = stroke.
• Unidirectional (beats same direction on all gazes) = peripheral.

TS — Test of Skew

Cover-uncover test. Cover one eye, then alternate. Watch for vertical drift when uncovered.

Skew Present = CENTRAL
No Skew = PERIPHERAL

• Vertical correction (eye moves up or down when uncovered) = brainstem/cerebellar stroke.
• Stable alignment (no vertical correction) = peripheral.

INFARCT Clinical Warning Rule
Any SINGLE "Central" finding on the 3-step exam requires treating the vertigo as a stroke (INFARCT):

• Impulse Normal (VOR intact)
• Nystagmus Fast-phase alternating (Direction-changing)
• Fast-Alternating Redirection of Cover (Test of Skew deviation present)

Obtain urgent Brain MRI with DWI. Keep patient NPO until swallow screening is completed.
Exam Step Peripheral (Neuritis) - Benign Central (Stroke) - DANGEROUS
Head Impulse (HIT) Abnormal VOR: Delayed catch-up corrective saccade back to target nose. Normal VOR: Eyes stay locked on target nose immediately with no saccade.
Nystagmus (N) Unidirectional: Beats away from lesion side (fast phase) in all gaze directions. Direction-changing: Beats left on left gaze, beats right on right gaze; or vertical.
Test of Skew (TS) Conjugate: Eyes stay in alignment; no vertical movement on uncover test. Skew Deviation: Vertical correction (uncovered eye shifts vertically to realign).
Summary ALL THREE steps must be benign to rule out stroke. Any ONE central finding overrides normal scans.
