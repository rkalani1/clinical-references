PERIPHERAL VESTIBULAR PROFILE

All three exam steps are benign (Abnormal HIT, Unidirectional Nystagmus, No Skew). This is highly consistent with acute peripheral vestibulopathy (e.g. vestibular neuritis). Manage symptomatically.

INFARCT Clinical Rule

Any SINGLE "Central" finding requires treating as a stroke (INFARCT):
• Impulse Normal (VOR intact)
• Fast-Alternating nystagmus (direction-changing)
• Refixation on Cover Test (skew deviation present)
• Note: **HINTS+** adds new unilateral hearing loss as a central stroke sign.

Acute Vestibular Syndrome (AVS) Triage Protocol

⚡ HINTS Core Diagnostic Matrix

Exam Step Peripheral (Neuritis) - Benign Central (Stroke) - Dangerous
Head Impulse (HIT) Abnormal VOR: Delayed catch-up corrective saccade back to target nose. Normal VOR: Eyes stay locked on target nose immediately with no saccades.
Nystagmus (N) Unidirectional: Beats away from lesion side (fast phase) in all gaze directions. Direction-changing: Beats left on left gaze, beats right on right gaze, or vertical.
Test of Skew (TS) Conjugate: Eyes stay in alignment; no vertical movement on uncover test. Skew Deviation: Vertical correction (uncovered eye shifts vertically to realign).

🎯 Target Patient Population (AVS)

The HINTS examination should only be applied to patients presenting with the Acute Vestibular Syndrome (AVS) clinical triad:

  • Acute, continuous vertigo lasting hours to days.
  • Spontaneous or gaze-evoked nystagmus.
  • Significant nausea, vomiting, and head-motion intolerance.
  • Confounder: Do not use HINTS in patients with episodic vertigo (e.g. BPPV) or patients without active nystagmus.

🚨 HINTS+ & INFARCT Clinical Warning Rule

Any SINGLE Central finding on the HINTS+ exam requires treating as an acute stroke:

  • Impulse Normal (VOR intact).
  • Fast-Alternating nystagmus (direction-changing).
  • Refixation on Cover Test (skew deviation present).
  • + Hearing Loss: New unilateral hearing impairment is a central sign (AICA stroke).
  • Clinical Note: HINTS+ is highly sensitive and specific. It is more sensitive than MRI DWI in the first 24–48 hours from vertigo onset.

📈 Landmark Evidence Summary

Study & Citation Cohort Key Metric / Finding Clinical Translation
Kattah et al. (Stroke, 2009) 101 AVS patients with high-risk features Sensitivity 100%, Specificity 96% for identifying stroke compared to initial MRI. Establish HINTS bedside exam superiority over early MRI DWI in hyperacute phase.
Newman-Toker et al. (Acad Emerg Med, 2013) 190 AVS patients in ED setting Missed stroke rate is extremely high if bedside HINTS is omitted or performed incorrectly. Proper technique and population selection (must have nystagmus) are mandatory.

References: [1] Kattah JC, et al. Stroke. 2009;40(11):3504-3510. [2] Newman-Toker DE, et al. Acad Emerg Med. 2013;20(10):986-996. [3] Kleindorfer DO, et al. 2021 AHA/ASA Stroke Prevention Guidelines.
