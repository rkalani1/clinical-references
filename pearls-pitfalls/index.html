Clinical Pearls & Trainee Pitfalls

Interactive checklist of high-yield pearls and trap mitigations

1. Acute Assessment

Diagnosis
✓
Time is Brain: ~1.9M neurons lost/min in LVO. Every minute saved saves 1.8 days of healthy life.
✓
NIHSS 0 Warning: Posterior strokes can present with severe ataxia/vertigo and score 0.
✓
Check Glucose First: Correct hypoglycemia (BG <60 mg/dL) before giving thrombolytics.
✓
Permissive HTN: SBP <220/120 is acceptable in AIS unless candidate for thrombolysis (target <185/110).
✓
CT Sensitivity: Non-contrast head CT is only ~25% sensitive in the first 6 hours. Normal CT does not rule out stroke.

2. Treatment Protocols

Therapeutics
✓
Tenecteplase Dosing: TNK 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg) single IV bolus. Logistically faster; no infusion required.
✓
Do Not Delay for CTP: Within the 4.5h window, do not wait for perfusion imaging to start lytics.
✓
POINT/CHANCE DAPT: Minor stroke (NIHSS ≤3) / high-risk TIA: load clopidogrel 300mg + ASA 325mg, then DAPT x 21d.
✓
Atorvastatin: Start high-intensity statin (Atorvastatin 80mg) immediately. Do not wait for fasting lipids.

3. Intracerebral Hemorrhage

Critical Care
✓
BP Target Timing: SBP <140 mmHg within 2 hours of onset reduces hematoma expansion (Nicardipine preferred).
✓
Avoid SBP <130: Intensive BP control (<130 mmHg) increases renal complications without functional benefit.
✓
Speed of Reversal: Reversing anticoagulants is the single most time-sensitive ICH intervention.
✓
CTA Spot Sign: Contrast extravasation within the hematoma indicates active bleeding and expansion risk.

4. Disposition & Safety

Disposition
✓
Dedicated Stroke Unit: Reduces mortality and dependency (NNT 18) regardless of stroke severity.
✓
Early Mobilization: Avoid bedrest beyond 24h, but avoid very early mobilization (<24h) which can harm.
✓
Swallow Screen PO Safety: Keep strictly NPO until bedside screen. Aspiration pneumonia is preventable.
✓
Normothermia Target: Every 1°C increase in the first 72h worsens outcomes. Target temp <37.8°C.

Trainee Pitfalls Assistant

QA Hub
1. Trace hematuria post-TNK? +
No reversal. Microscopic hematuria is expected and not an indication to reverse tenecteplase. Only gross hematuria, active bleeding requiring transfusion, or symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage warrants reversal. Continue monitoring.

Ref: AHA/ASA 2019 AIS Guidelines

2. High ICH Score = Comfort Care recommendation? +
No. The ICH Score predicts 30-day mortality, not long-term functional recovery, and was derived from historical cohorts with high rates of early DNR. Guidelines recommend postponing new DNR orders for at least 48 hours to avoid self-fulfilling mortality prophecy. Use FUNC Score for functional predictions.

Ref: AHA/ASA ICH Guidelines 2022; Rost 2008

3. CVT with hemorrhage = Hold heparin? +
No. Anticoagulation is indicated even in the presence of venous hemorrhagic transformation. The hemorrhage is caused by venous congestion (outflow block), and heparin treats the underlying thrombosis. AHA/ASA recommends initial anticoagulation with heparin (UFH or LMWH) as Class IIa.

Ref: AHA/ASA CVT Guidelines 2024

4. NIHSS 3 but M1 Occlusion = Skip EVT? +
Do not automatically skip. Low NIHSS does not exclude benefit. Up to 30% of LVO patients with initially low NIHSS deteriorate neurologically when managed medically. Discuss urgently with the neurointerventionalist. Early EVT may prevent devastating progression.

Ref: MR CLEAN-LATE 2023; ESCAPE trial

5. Seizure at onset = TNK contraindicated? +
No. Seizure at onset is no longer an absolute contraindication if residual deficits are clearly ischemic and not Todd's paralysis. If clinical uncertainty exists, CT perfusion or CTA can help differentiate. Do a thorough exam to confirm stroke deficits.

Ref: AHA/ASA 2019 Guidelines; ACT-FAST 2022

6. Spontaneous ICH = Start prophylactic AED? +
No. Prophylactic anti-seizure medications are NOT recommended (Class III: No Benefit). Only treat clinical seizures or non-convulsive status epilepticus diagnosed on EEG. Prophylactic AEDs are associated with worse functional recovery and unnecessary drug-drug interactions.

Ref: AHA/ASA ICH Guidelines 2022

7. Clopidogrel + Omeprazole interaction? +
Yes, significant. Omeprazole and esomeprazole are strong CYP2C19 inhibitors that significantly reduce clopidogrel's conversion to its active metabolite, potentially increasing stroke risk. Switch to pantoprazole (weak inhibitor) or an H2 blocker.

Ref: FDA Safety Communication 2009; COGENT trial

8. New hemianopia 3h post-TNK = HT? +
Get STAT NCHCT, but consider re-occlusion. Hemorrhagic transformation (HT) usually presents with headache, decreased arousal, and severe hypertension. A new isolated focal deficit without arousal changes suggests vessel re-occlusion. If CT is negative for blood, proceed to urgent CTA; the patient may need rescue EVT.

Ref: Clinical reasoning; AHA/ASA 2019

9. Use phenytoin for post-stroke seizures? +
Avoid. Phenytoin has severe drug interactions with DOACs, statins, and steroids, can cause hypotension, and is linked to worse long-term cognitive recovery. Levetiracetam (Keppra) or Lacosamide (Vimpat) are preferred first-line agents.

Ref: Clinical consensus; AHA/ASA 2022

10. Malignant MCA age >60 = Automatic futility? +
No, but requires explicit shared decisions. DESTINY II trial showed that hemicraniectomy in age >60 significantly increases survival (NNT 2) but most survivors have moderate-to-severe disability (mRS 4-5). This is a goals-of-care decision rather than medical futility. Talk to the family about patient values.

Ref: DESTINY II trial (2014)

Clinical Pearls & Management Pitfalls Cheatsheet

Essential Ward & ICU Reference for Stroke Trainees

🧠 Acute Assessment & Diagnosis Pearls

  • Time is Brain: ~1.9 million neurons lost per minute in untreated large vessel occlusion (LVO) strokes.
  • NIHSS 0 Alert: Posterior strokes (cerebellar/brainstem) can present with severe vertigo/ataxia and score 0.
  • Check Glucose: Correct hypoglycemia (<60 mg/dL) before administering lytics to rule out stroke mimic.
  • Permissive HTN: Keep SBP <220/120 mmHg in AIS. Keep <185/110 mmHg if giving thrombolytics.
  • CT Sensitivity: Non-contrast Head CT is only ~25% sensitive for ischemia in the first 6 hours. Normal CT does not rule out stroke.

💊 Therapeutics & Treatment Pearls

  • TNK Dosing: Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg (max 25 mg) single IV bolus is preferred over alteplase.
  • Do Not Delay for CTP: Perfusion imaging should not delay lytic administration in <4.5h window.
  • POINT/CHANCE DAPT: Minor stroke (NIHSS ≤3) / high-risk TIA: clopidogrel 300mg load + ASA 325mg load, then DAPT for 21 days.
  • Statins: Initiate Atorvastatin 80mg immediately in-hospital; do not delay for fasting lipid panels.
  • ELAN DOAC Timing: Days 1-4 for minor/mod, Days 6-7 for severe ischemic stroke with AFib.

🔴 Intracerebral Hemorrhage Pearls

  • BP Target Timing: SBP <140 mmHg within 2 hours of onset reduces hematoma expansion. Nicardipine preferred.
  • Avoid SBP <130: Intensive BP control (<130 mmHg) increases renal injury risk without functional benefits.
  • Speed of Reversal: Reversing anticoagulants is the single most time-sensitive ICH intervention.
  • CTA Spot Sign: Contrast extravasation represents active bleeding and predicts expansion.

🏥 Care, Safety & Disposition Pearls

  • Dedicated Stroke Unit: Reduces mortality and dependency (NNT 18) regardless of stroke severity.
  • Early Mobilization: Avoid bedrest beyond 24h, but avoid very early mobilization (<24h - AVERT showed harm).
  • Swallow Screen: Keep strictly NPO until bedside screen. Aspiration pneumonia is preventable.
  • Normothermia Target: Keep temperature <37.8°C. Fever in first 72h is associated with worse outcomes.
