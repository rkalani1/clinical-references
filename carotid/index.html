Diagnostic Imaging Modalities

Modality Advantages Limitations / Overestimations Sensitivity
CE-MRA Contrast-enhanced MRA is more accurate than TOF. Highly sensitive. TOF-MRA overestimates degree of stenosis due to signal loss from turbulent flow. ~99%
CTA Accurately shows calcifications, surrounding bone, and soft tissue anatomy. High calcification can obscure lumen, requiring multiplanar reconstruction. ~97%
Duplex U/S Non-invasive, fast, bedside screening tool. Very operator-dependent. Overestimates if contralateral carotid is occluded. Variable
DSA (Cath) Gold standard diagnostic tool. Best spatial resolution. Invasive, carries inherent 1-2% risk of stroke/procedural complications. 100%
Revascularization Modality Selection • **Timing**: Revascularization should ideally occur **within 2 weeks** of TIA/stroke. Emergent revascularization (<2 days) is not recommended.
• **Age >75**: CEA is preferred over transfemoral stenting (CAS) because CAS carries double the risk of periprocedural stroke in the elderly.
• **CAS Indications**: Restenosis after CEA, radiation-induced stenosis, surgically inaccessible plaque, or severe cardiac disease.
• **TCAR Advantage**: Trans-carotid access with flow reversal shows significantly lower periprocedural stroke/death risk compared to transfemoral stenting.
Intensive Medical Management • **Antiplatelet**: Short-term DAPT (ASA+Clopidogrel) for minor stroke/high-risk TIA. Long-term single antiplatelet.
• **Blood Pressure**: Gradual titration to a long-term target SBP <130/80 mmHg (avoid diuretics initially in bilateral high-grade stenosis).
• **Lipids**: Target LDL-C <70 mg/dL (SPARCL trial) using high-intensity statin ± Ezetimibe.
• **Diabetes**: Glycemic control targeting HbA1c ≤7.0%.
For educational & clinical decision support. Verify local institutional protocols prior to application.
