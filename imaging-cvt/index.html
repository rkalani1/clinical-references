Imaging Follow-Up Protocols

Standardized follow-up imaging schedule to prevent missed scans and diagnostics.

Acute Ischemic Stroke (Post-TNK)

24 Hours Post-TNK: Non-contrast Head CT (NCHCT) is required to screen for asymptomatic hemorrhagic transformation prior to initiating antiplatelets/anticoagulants.
24-48 Hours: Brain MRI DWI/FLAIR (if not obtained on admission) to confirm infarct territory, count/size, and rule out silent ischemic lesions.
Discharge Planning: Ensure vascular imaging (CTA head/neck or MRA head/neck) is complete. If not, schedule outpatient vascular imaging within 1 week.
Outpatient (3-6 Months): If significant carotid/vertebral stenosis is managed medically, repeat MRA or CTA to evaluate for plaque progression/restenosis.

Acute Ischemic Stroke (Post-EVT)

Immediate Post-Procedure: Flat-panel CT in angiography suite to assess for immediate contrast extravasation vs. hyperacute intracranial bleeding.
24 Hours Post-Procedure: NCHCT +/- CTA head to confirm successful vessel recanalization, check device/stent patency, and rule out delayed hemorrhage.
48-72 Hours: Brain MRI DWI to evaluate final infarct volume and core necrosis size for long-term prognosis.
Outpatient (3-6 Months): If an intracranial stent was placed (e.g. carotid or MCA stenting), schedule CTA or MRA to monitor for in-stent stenosis.

Intracerebral Hemorrhage (ICH)

6 Hours Post-Onset: NCHCT head to monitor for hematoma expansion. Expansion of ≥33% or ≥6 mL is clinically significant and predicts poor outcome.
24 Hours Post-Onset: Repeat NCHCT if clinically stable. Order CTA if spot sign or secondary vascular etiology (AVM, aneurysm) is suspected.
Subacute (Hospitalization): For lobar, young, or non-hypertensive patients, obtain Brain MRI (GRE/SWI) + contrast to screen for cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), cavernoma, or tumor.
Outpatient (3 Months): Repeat Brain MRI or NCHCT to confirm complete hematoma resorption and check for new microbleeds (GRE/SWI).

Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (SAH)

Day 0 (Admission): CTA head/neck to identify ruptured aneurysm. If negative, perform conventional Digital Subtraction Angiography (DSA).
Days 3-14 (Daily): Transcranial Doppler (TCD) measurements daily to screen for vasospasm (peak vasospasm risk occurs on Days 7-10).
Days 7-10: Repeat DSA or CTA head to rule out delayed vasospasm or check coiling/clipping occlusion integrity.
Outpatient (3-6 Months): Repeat MRA head or CTA head to screen for aneurysm recurrence, neck remnant, or de novo aneurysms.
Neurological Decline Rule
Any acute change in neurologic examination requires a STAT non-contrast head CT (NCHCT).
If post-TNK, immediately suspend all antithrombotics and prepare lytic reversal protocols. If post-EVT, evaluate for re-occlusion (STAT CTA/MRA) in addition to hemorrhage screening.

Cerebral Venous Thrombosis

Inpatient monitoring and long-term outpatient follow-up checklists for CVT.

1. Imaging Follow-Up

  • • Schedule: Repeat MRI/MRV or CTV at 3 to 6 months post-diagnosis to assess for venous recanalization.
  • • Acute Worsening: Repeat venous imaging immediately if there is new severe headache, seizures, or focal deficits.
  • • Contraindications: If MRI is contraindicated (e.g. pacemakers), CTV is an acceptable alternative for recanalization follow-up.
  • • D-dimer: Serial D-dimer is not recommended for guiding treatment duration, but can support baseline diagnostics.

2. ICP Monitoring

  • • Visual Screens: Fundoscopy must be performed at baseline and follow-up to monitor for papilledema.
  • • Acuity Testing: Formally assess visual acuity and visual fields at baseline to detect impending optic nerve compromise.
  • • Lumbar Puncture: LP opening pressure check is indicated if pseudotumor-like presentation is suspected (OP >25 cmH₂O).
  • • Acetazolamide: Start 250-500 mg PO BID to lower CSF production if papilledema or progressive visual loss is documented.

3. Thrombophilia Workup

  • • Inherited Panel: Factor V Leiden, Prothrombin G20210A mutation, Protein C, Protein S, and Antithrombin III activities.
  • • Acquired Panel: Antiphospholipid antibodies (Lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin IgG/IgM, anti-β2-glycoprotein 1 IgG/IgM).
  • • Myeloproliferative: Check JAK2 V617F mutation if polycythemia or thrombocytosis is present without clear cause.
  • • Critical Timing Warning: Defer Protein C, Protein S, and Antithrombin testing during acute heparin/warfarin therapy. Re-test 2-4 weeks after stopping anticoagulants to avoid false positives.

4. Seizure Management

  • • No Prophylaxis: Routine prophylactic anti-seizure medications are not recommended by AHA/ASA.
  • • Acute Treatment: Treat acute symptomatic seizures immediately (e.g. Levetiracetam 500-1000 mg IV).
  • • Therapy Duration: Continue anti-seizure medication for 3 to 6 months in patients with a single acute symptomatic seizure.
  • • EEG Monitoring: Obtain continuous or routine EEG if the patient has persistent altered mental status to rule out subclinical status.
Anticoagulation Rule in Hemorrhagic CVT
Anticoagulation is indicated for acute CVT even in the presence of pre-existing hemorrhagic venous infarction.
The hemorrhage is driven by venous hypertension from outflow obstruction. Halting heparin allows thrombus progression and worsens bleeding. Maintain standard therapeutic Heparin infusion or LMWH.
