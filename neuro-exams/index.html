Differentiating Delirium from Aphasia

Feature Delirium (Acute Confusion) Aphasia
Onset & Course Acute/subacute. **Fluctuating** with lucid intervals. Sudden onset. Stable deficits. Preserved arousal.
Attention Severely Impaired. Difficulty focusing or shifting. Preserved attention. Focuses on examiner.
Language Rambling, incoherent, tangential. Content is illogical. Specific deficits (paraphasias, impaired repetition/naming).
L.O.C. Fluctuates from hyperalert to lethargic. Visual hallucinations common. Usually alert. Preserved awareness.
Focal Signs Absent (unless metabolic flap or pre-existing stroke). Commonly has hemiplegia or sensory loss (left MCA).

Bedside Coma Exam Protocol

1. Level of Consciousness & Motor

• Check response to voice, touch, and central pain (sternal rub, supraorbital pressure).

• Motor Responses: Localizing (crosses midline), Withdrawal (pulls away), Decorticate (elbow flexion), Decerebrate (extension + pronation).

2. Brainstem Reflexes & Breathing

• Pupillary Light Reflex: CN II/III. Reactivity and anisocoria (uncal herniation sign).

• Corneal (CN V/VII) & Oculocephalic (Doll's eyes - CN III, VI, VIII).

• Breathing: Cheyne-Stokes (forebrain), Apneustic (pons gasps), Ataxic/Biot's (medulla - terminal).

⚡ Delirium vs. Aphasia Differential Table

Feature Acute Confusional State (Delirium) Aphasia
Definition Transient, fluctuating disturbance in attention and awareness. Acquired language-dominant hemisphere disorder.
Onset / Course Acute/subacute (hours/days). **Fluctuating** symptoms. Sudden onset. Stable deficits. Alert.
Attention Severely Impaired. Inability to focus, sustain, or shift attention. Generally preserved (except in large neglect/hemispheric overload).
Language Rambling, incoherent, tangential. Content is illogical. Specific deficits (paraphasias, impaired repetition/naming).
L.O.C. / Hallucination Fluctuates. Visual hallucinations common. Alert. Hallucinations are atypical.
Focal Signs Absent (unless pre-existing). Right hemiparesis/hemiplegia common (left MCA).

🧠 Bedside Coma Exam Checklist

1. LOC & Motor Responses:

  • Verify response to voice, touch, and central pain (sternal rub, nailbed).
  • Motor: Localizing, Withdrawal, Decorticate, Decerebrate, Flaccid.

2. Brainstem Reflexes & Breathing:

  • Pupillary Light Reflex (CN II/III). Anisocoria: Uncal herniation.
  • Corneals (CN V/VII) & Doll's Eyes (CN III, VI, VIII).
  • Breathing: Cheyne-Stokes (forebrain), Apneustic (pons), Ataxic (medullary).
