Delirium vs. Aphasia & Bedside Coma Exam

Systematic differentiation guides and structured exam checklists for acute stroke and neurocritical care encounters.

Differentiating Delirium (Acute Confusional State) from Aphasia

Feature Acute Confusional State (Delirium) Aphasia
Definition Transient, fluctuating disturbance in attention, awareness, and global cognition. Acquired language-dominant hemisphere disorder affecting language processing.
Onset & Course Acute/subacute (hours to days). **Hallmark: Fluctuating symptoms** with lucid intervals. Sudden onset. Mental status stable; language deficits stable or slowly recovering.
Attention Severely Impaired. Difficulty focusing, sustaining, or shifting. (Digit span, months backward). Generally preserved (except in large stroke causing neglect or sensory overload).
Language Rambling, incoherent, tangential. Content is illogical or nonsensical. Specific linguistic deficits (paraphasias, neologisms, impaired naming/repetition).
L.O.C. & Hallucinations Ranges from hyperalert to lethargic. Visual hallucinations common. Usually alert. Hallucinations are atypical.
Focal Signs Absent (unless prior deficits or global metabolic flap). Commonly associated with right-sided hemiplegia or sensory loss (focal left MCA).

Bedside Coma Examination Checklist

1. Level of Consciousness & Motor • **Arousal**: Response to voice, tactile stimulation, and central pain (sternal rub, supraorbital pressure, nailbed pressure).
• **Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)**: Complete scoring of Eye (1–4), Verbal (1–5), and Motor (1–6).
• **Motor Responses**:
  – *Localizing*: Arm moves to cross midline toward painful stimulus.
  – *Withdrawal*: Limb pulls away from pain.
  – *Decorticate Posturing*: Flexion of elbow, wrist, and shoulder with adduction.
  – *Decerebrate Posturing*: Extension and pronation of upper limb.
2. Brainstem Reflexes & Breathing • **Pupillary Light Reflex**: Size, shape, reactivity (CN II & III). *Anisocoria indicates uncal herniation.* • **Corneal Reflex**: Direct corneal touch with cotton swab (CN V & VII). • **Oculocephalic (Doll's Eyes)**: Rapid horizontal turning of head in absence of cervical spine injury (CN III, VI, VIII). • **Oculovestibular (Cold Caloric)**: Injection of ice water into external canal in intact tympanic membrane. • **Breathing Patterns**:
  – *Cheyne-Stokes*: Alternating hyperpnea & apnea (forebrain/diencephalon).
  – *Apneustic*: Deep, gasping inspiration w/ pause (pons).
  – *Ataxic / Biot's*: Irregular, disorganized breaths (medulla).
