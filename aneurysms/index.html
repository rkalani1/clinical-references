Unruptured Cerebral Aneurysms

Epidemiology, rupture risk assessment (PHASES score), diagnostic modalities, and re-imaging intervals.

Classification & Distribution • **Saccular (Berry)**: Thin/absent media, fragmented internal elastic lamina. Accounts for most aSAH.
• **Fusiform**: Dilation of the entire arterial circumference, highly associated with atherosclerosis.
• **Mycotic**: Infective, saccular or fusiform. Located on distal branches of anterior circulation.
• **Distribution**: Solitary in 70–75%; multiple in 20–30%.
• **Location**: Anterior circulation bifurcations of the Circle of Willis (85%).
• **CN III Palsy**: Mass effect from posterior communicating junction can compress CN III, causing a **pupil-involving third nerve palsy** (surgical emergency).
Epidemiology & Screening • **Prevalence**: ~3% in the general population. Post-50y shows strong female predominance (2:1) due to estrogen deficiency and reduced collagen.
• **Genetic Syndromes**: Autosomal Dominant Polycystic Kidney Disease (AD-PCKD: **7x increased risk**), Ehlers-Danlos, Moyamoya.
• **Asymptomatic Screening**: Indicated if ≥2 first-degree relatives have intracranial aneurysms or SAH, or if a high-risk syndromic association exists.

Diagnostic Modalities & Re-imaging Schedule

Diagnostic Modality Accuracy • **DSA**: Gold standard. Best resolution for planning treatments.
• **MRA / CTA**: Lower accuracy for very small aneurysms (<4mm).
  – *3T TOF-MRA*: Sensitivity 99%, Specificity 94%.
  – *CTA*: Sensitivity 97%, Specificity 98%.
• **Bedside Tip**: If noninvasive studies are negative but clinical pretest probability remains high (e.g. sentinel headache, CN palsy), obtain a conventional DSA.
Monitoring Re-imaging Intervals • **Re-imaging Schedule**: For unruptured, untreated aneurysms managed conservatively:
  – Obtain follow-up MRA or CTA **6 months** after initial diagnosis.
  – If stable, obtain **annually** for 2–3 years.
  – If stable, transition to **q2–3 years** or longer.
• **Antithrombotics**:
  – *Aspirin*: Safe and appears to reduce risk of aneurysm inflammation/growth (NASCET post-hoc).
  – *Anticoagulation*: May exacerbate SAH severity. Perform careful risk/benefit analysis.
General Indications for Intervention (Clipping vs. Coiling): • **Intervention is indicated for**: Symptomatic aneurysms (mass effect, cranial neuropathy) or large size (≥ 7–10 mm).
• **Consider intervention for**: Basilar tip location, young patient age, coexisting aneurysms with prior SAH, or documented growth on serial monitoring scans.
• **Treatment Options**: Endovascular coiling / flow diversion (lower periprocedural morbidity) vs. Surgical clipping (lower rates of aneurysm recurrence/regrowth).
